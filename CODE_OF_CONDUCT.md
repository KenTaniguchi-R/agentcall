# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people want to work with. Disagree about the code as much as you
like; do not make it about the person.

## What is expected

- Argue from evidence. "This is wrong because X" beats "this is wrong", and both
  beat "you are wrong".
- Assume the other person read the thing and reached a different conclusion,
  rather than that they did not read it.
- Accept a decision once it is made, or reopen it with new evidence. Not both,
  and not forever.
- Say when you are unsure. Confident wrongness costs everyone more time than an
  admitted gap.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments; sexual attention or imagery;
publishing someone's private information; sustained disruption of discussion;
and any of the above dressed up as a joke or as "just being direct".

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code comments, and any
space where you are representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem privately through
[GitHub Security Advisories](https://github.com/KenTaniguchi-R/agentcall/security/advisories/new)
— it is a private channel to the maintainers, and it works for conduct reports
even though the form is labelled for vulnerabilities. Include what happened and
where. Reports stay between you and the maintainers.

Maintainers will respond with a warning, a temporary block, or a permanent ban,
proportionate to what happened and to whether it is a pattern. Maintainers who
break these rules face the same consequences as anyone else, more visibly rather
than less.

If you believe an enforcement decision was wrong, say so in the same channel.
You will get a real answer, not a form reply.
